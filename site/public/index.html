Why it exists

For months, on a real PC with its antivirus switched on and up to date, a program sat set to start at every sign-in. It was signed by a real software company, so it looked safe. Next to it were planted files: one changed after signing, three unsigned, and hidden payloads dressed up as sound files. Nothing flagged it. Hideout did, on its first scan — and flagged nothing else among 465 things set to start by themselves.

Your PC

  • Quick scan — everything set to start by itself, checked for the trick malware uses most: a trusted, signed program with planted files beside it.
  • Deep check — 16 areas: tasks, services, drivers, security settings, network connections, browser extensions and more.
  • Only looks. It never deletes, moves, disables or uploads anything. Clean-up goes through Windows Security, step by step.
  • Not checked is never shown as clean. If something can't be checked, Hideout says so.

Money

  • Import a statement you download from your bank's website. It stays on your PC.
  • Every repeating charge, how much it costs a month, and when the price went up.
  • Still charging after you cancelled? Hideout flags it with the dates — and a date to tell your bank by.
  • Since the scam: every transfer and every new company that took money since the day it happened.
  • Honest refund odds for 46 common companies, taken from their own policies. When nobody knows, it says so.
  • How to cancel — the company's own steps, the traps named in advance ("pause" still bills you), and a button to the company's real cancel page.

The guide

  • An AI assistant, powered by Claude, that walks you through what to do, one step at a time, in plain words. It says it's an AI, and it can be wrong.
  • It remembers where you left off — which steps are done, what you cancelled and when.
  • Writes the letters: refund requests, cancellations, bank disputes. Anything it can't know from you goes in [brackets] for you to confirm or delete.
  • You send them. Hideout never logs into your accounts, never cancels anything and never sends email for you.
  • Warns about the second scam: anyone offering to "recover" your money for a fee or remote access.

What Hideout promises

It only looks

No deleting, moving, disabling or uploading. It can't change your PC, and neither can the guide.

Hands off your accounts

It never asks for passwords, never logs in anywhere, never cancels or sends anything for you.

Memory locked to your PC

Your case file and statements are stored encrypted, with a key sealed by your PC's security chip. Nothing is uploaded to be stored.

The guide sees less than you do

When you ask the guide, it gets a summary with your name, account numbers and PC name removed — and nothing else from your PC.

One door out

The app can reach exactly one address on the internet: the guide's service. Everything else is refused.

Open to check

The code is public. Anyone can read what it does — and what it doesn't.

Refund odds and "tell your bank by" dates are general information, not legal or financial advice. Your bank's own terms decide. Hideout isn't affiliated with any bank or company it lists.
